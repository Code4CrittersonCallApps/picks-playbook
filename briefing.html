5 · Macro fibs for swing trades (BCB style)

  1. Pick the anchor on the WEEKLY or MONTHLY chart: the cycle low to the cycle high (the full impulse). Never anchor on a random daily wiggle.
  2. Draw the retrace from high back to low: .382 (shallow, strong trend), .5, .618 (golden pocket starts), .702 / .786 (BCB's levels for a deep but healthy pullback). Below .786, the move is usually over.
  3. Look for confluence: a fib level plus old support/resistance plus the 50W/200W MA. Two or more lining up = a real level.
  4. Entry: a weekly close holding the level (a wick through is fine). Stop: below the next fib down. Targets: the prior high, then extensions 1.272 and 1.618.
  5. Swing version: repeat on the daily chart using the latest swing (low to high) for tighter entries inside the macro map.

6 · Russell clarification (correcting what I said before)

SCHA vs IWM, same daydaily-return correlation
SCHA vs IWM, 1-day lag≈ 0 = no delay

The Schwab product you remember is almost certainly SCHA (Schwab U.S. Small-Cap ETF). It tracks small caps the same day; it's a different index (Dow Jones U.S. Small-Cap Total Stock Market), not the Russell 2000, but it moves nearly 1:1 with IWM. No product follows the Russell 1:1 on a delay. If one did, everyone would front-run it. SWSSX (Schwab Small-Cap Index mutual fund) only prices once a day at the close. That's a pricing schedule, not a lag in tracking.

What IS real, and separate: pump-timing tendencies. After a BTC breakout in a bull regime, MSTR has followed in a median of ~6 days and AVAX ~16 days (Correlation Lab, n = 9 and 4 events). Those are tendencies in when different assets move, not fund tracking, and they're loose.
